Understanding these numbers

What does "±" mean, and why does it matter?

These estimates come from the American Community Survey, which asks a sample of households each year -- not every household. Because it's a sample, every number here is an estimate with some built-in uncertainty, not an exact head-count.

The ± number next to an estimate is its margin of error (MOE). It's the range the true value most likely falls in. For example, "10.2% ± 0.7%" means the real figure is probably somewhere between 9.5% and 10.9% -- not exactly 10.2%.

Why be cautious: smaller counties and smaller population groups get surveyed less, so their estimates carry wider margins of error -- less precision. The colored dot next to each number flags how much to trust it:

  • reliable margin of error is small; treat the number as fairly precise.
  • caution margin of error is sizeable; treat the number as approximate.
  • unreliable margin of error is very large; treat the number as a rough signal, not a fact.

Rule of thumb: if two counties' ranges (value ± MOE) overlap, don't assume one is really higher or lower than the other -- the difference could just be survey noise. Hover any ± number or dot throughout this report for a plain-language explanation of that specific value.
